Research Question

I wanted to understand which businesses benefit when AI requires more computing capacity. Instead of focusing only on chipmakers, I looked at the physical infrastructure behind data centers, especially power systems, cooling, and maintenance.

Industry View

Growth drivers

  • AI and high-performance computing: More powerful chips create higher rack density and greater power and heat-management needs.
  • Liquid cooling adoption: Traditional air cooling becomes less effective as computing density rises, creating demand for coolant distribution and related systems.
  • Cloud and colocation expansion: Large operators need reliable infrastructure that can scale without increasing downtime risk.
  • Grid constraints: Limited power availability can increase demand for more efficient systems and on-site power solutions.

Risks and headwinds

  • Data-center construction and customer capital spending can be cyclical.
  • Manufacturing expansion creates execution and supply-chain risk.
  • Fast technology changes could make current cooling designs less competitive.
  • Strong growth expectations can create valuation risk even when the business performs well.

Company Example: Vertiv

Vertiv sells critical digital infrastructure, including power-management, thermal-management, modular, and service solutions. That gives the company exposure to data-center investment without depending on one specific chip or software platform.

Why it stands out

Vertiv can sell across multiple parts of a data center and then provide ongoing services. Its 2025 results reported organic orders growth of 25%, showing how quickly demand was expanding. The company also announced additional manufacturing capacity for AI-ready cooling solutions in 2026.

My view

The strongest part of the thesis is that power and cooling are necessary regardless of which AI model or semiconductor company wins. The main question is whether future growth is already reflected in the stock's valuation. My next step would be to compare Vertiv's margins, backlog growth, and valuation multiples with Eaton, Schneider Electric, and other infrastructure peers.
