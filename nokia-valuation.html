Project Overview

Valuation Approach

Trading multiples

The exercise used LTM EBITDA of about $2.833 billion and NTM EBITDA of about $3.602 billion. The lower forward multiple reflects the increase in expected EBITDA. That helped me see why the same enterprise value can look more or less expensive depending on whether I use historical or forecast results.

Discounted cash flow

The DCF produced an estimated value of $10.10 per share compared with an observed price of $10.70 in the exercise. That implied approximately 5.6% downside at that point. The terminal value represented 87.9% of enterprise value, which made the result especially sensitive to the discount rate and terminal assumptions.

Main sensitivity

The most important takeaway was not the exact price target. It was that a small change in WACC, terminal growth, or the exit multiple could materially change the result because so much value came from the terminal period.

Conclusion

Based on the assumptions in the exercise, Nokia appeared close to fairly valued but slightly above the DCF estimate. The gap was not large enough for me to treat the result as a clear investment signal. I would want to test different operating cases and compare Nokia with a stronger peer set before reaching a final view.

This is a student valuation project for educational purposes and is not investment advice.
